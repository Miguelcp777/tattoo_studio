# Task: The ink reads the photograph

## 1. Problem / requested change

Owner feedback on a real render: the fresh-ink reddening is invisible, and the tattoo looks
flat. Implements ADR-0014.

## 2. Current behavior

- The fresh-ink halo is a dilation of the ink coverage, so it covers the strokes as well as the
  skin. Measured: 3.6 % of pixels changed, peaking at +17 levels of red-minus-blue.
  Evidence: VERIFIED.
- The cylindrical warp applies only to limbs. The owner's job records `bodyPart: upper_back`,
  `curvature: None`, `taper: None`: a plain planar multiply with no surface term at all.
  Evidence: VERIFIED, read from the job's own stored transform.

## 3. Desired behavior

The reddening reads as recent without tinting the design, and the ink follows the body's form in
every zone, without the artwork being deformed.

## 4. Scope

`services/worker/mockup/engine.py`, `services/worker/app/studio.py`,
`contracts/schemas/studio-status.schema.json` and the regenerated artifacts, and a new test file.

## 5. Out of scope

Depth estimation and segmentation, which remain the standing intent in `mockup.spec.md`. The
catalogue-reference defect, which is TASK-0030.

## 6. Affected anchors

ADR-0014 (implements), MOCKUP-INV-001 (the reason displacement was rejected), PROD-INV-003,
ADR-0007.

## 7. Requirements

- **REQ-001** — The fresh-ink warmth lands on skin around the strokes, not on the pigment.
- **REQ-002** — Surface form is expressed as ink attenuation and never as displacement; the
  artwork's geometry is untouched.
- **REQ-003** — The attenuation is read from the photograph's own shading, so it applies to every
  zone rather than only the cylinder's limbs.
- **REQ-004** — Both terms are bounded and recorded in the transform.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

- **AC-001** (REQ-001) — On a mid-grey design, the band just outside the strokes reddens by more
  than 8 levels while the stroke's core stays under 1 level on average.
- **AC-002** (REQ-002) — More than 97 % of the design's silhouette is unmoved between a flat
  render and an attenuated one, at identical pixel dimensions.
- **AC-003** (REQ-003) — On a plate lit from one side, the falloff is stronger on the side
  turning away; on a uniform plate there is no falloff at all.
- **AC-004** (REQ-004) — A surface value outside 0..1 raises; the transform carries `surface` and
  `freshness`, and omits `surface` when it is zero.

## 9. Design approach

See ADR-0014. The displacement field was built first, measured on a real render, and rejected:
at any strength that reads as curvature it deforms the subject.

## 10. API / data / UI impact

The studio-status `transform` gains `surface` and `freshness`. Additive; both are optional.

## 11. Migration and backward compatibility

Renders made before this differ from renders made after, visibly in version history. Nothing
stored becomes invalid.

## 12. Test plan

Seven tests over the ring, the invariance of the artwork's silhouette, the falloff's dependence
on the photograph, and the bounds. The mechanism is asserted directly where a rendered-pixel
proxy would confound the effect with the plate's own gradient.

## 13. Risks and rollback

The attenuation depends on whatever lighting the photograph has, so the improvement is uneven.
Rollback is `surface=0`, which restores the previous render exactly.

## 14. Implementation checklist

- [x] Ring halo, with `DEFAULT_FRESHNESS = 2.0`.
- [x] `surface_falloff`, with `DEFAULT_SURFACE = 0.35`, applied to every zone.
- [x] Displacement field built, measured and removed.
- [x] Contract: `surface` and `freshness` on the transform; artifacts regenerated.
- [x] `mockup/tests/test_skin_finish.py`, 7 tests.
- [x] Specs updated: `mockup`, `contracts`.

## 15. Decision log

- **DEC-001** — Raising the freshness multiplier alone was tried and rejected on sight: at 2.8×
  the whole animal went warm, because the halo covers the strokes and mid-grey shading lets the
  tint through. Subtracting the coverage is the fix; the multiplier is secondary.
- **DEC-002** — The displacement field was implemented, wired into the mesh, and then deleted.
  Recorded because the negative result is the useful part: it is the obvious approach and it
  fails against MOCKUP-INV-001, not against taste.
- **DEC-003** — The back and chest were not added to the cylinder set. A back is a broad convex
  surface with the spine's valley through it, not a tube; it would look worse for being more
  confident.
- **DEC-004** — Two acceptance tests initially measured rendered pixels and failed, because the
  test plate's own gradient confounds the effect. They now assert the falloff directly. The code
  was right and the measurement was wrong, which is worth recording in a project whose rule is
  to correct the right artifact.

## 16. Evidence ledger

- **ev-001** — Before measurement, VERIFIED 2026-09-23: the owner's stored job transform reads
  `curvature: None`, `taper: None` for `upper_back`; the freshness effect covered 3.6 % of pixels
  at +17 levels peak.
- **ev-002** — Displacement trial, VERIFIED 2026-09-23: rendered at ±0.06 and +0.03 of the
  design's width on the owner's own job. At ±0.06 the animal's muzzle and ears warp visibly; at
  +0.03 the change is negligible. Rejected.
- **ev-003** — Attenuation trial, VERIFIED 2026-09-23: rendered at 0.35 and 0.55 on the same job.
  At 0.35 the design is intact and the ink grades with the body's light; above roughly 0.5 it
  reads as faded.
- **ev-004** — Verification run, 2026-09-23. `services/worker`: `pytest -q` **356 passed** (349
  before); ruff, format, mypy clean over 62 files. `contracts/python`: 117 passed. TypeScript
  lint, typecheck and tests clean. Guard `--baseline` exit 0. Status: VERIFIED by execution.
- **NOT_RUN** — No render on dark skin. Every background exercised so far has been light-skinned,
  and the luminance range there is compressed in a way that may misread the form.
- **NOT_RUN** — No tattooer has judged whether attenuated ink reads as curved or as faded.
